## What is proven

**Player state has exactly one receipted writer.** Deterministic handlers
either claim a mutation as an authoritative effect or defer the turn to the
narrator; the effect pipeline commits it and emits the receipt. The
receipt-vs-state agreement gates passed in *every* live run that produced a
report (10+ runs). Removals carry the resolved row, currency breaks larger
coins and receipts the effective per-denomination movement.

**Arriving somewhere restores its known inhabitants.** `rescope_scene` only
dropped; nothing put anyone back, so returning to a tavern found it empty.
`residents_of` + `hydrate_residents` fixed it: resident restoration went
2/4 → 5/5 live once location paraphrases resolved through aliases.

That 5/5 then drifted to **2 of 3 runs FAILING** (20260726_0113–0116), because
hydration can only restore what the graph still records — and three separate
writes were destroying the residency edge it reads. All three are closed and
the gate is **8/8** live (20260726_0207–0217), with each failure shape
exercised in at least one of those runs rather than merely absent:

- **Batch ops were re-sorted by type**, so the bridge's remove-then-add
  relocate ran add-then-remove and the wildcard removal deleted the edge just
  written — leaving the NPC with *no* residency. `apply_operations` now hoists
  only `AddNode` and keeps producer order.
- **Paraphrased places forked a second node** (`the-copper-finch`), so an
  `npc_updates[].location` moved residency somewhere hydration never looks.
  The bridge now resolves place names through `resolve_location_node`.
- **Removals were campaign-wide, not scene-scoped.** The extractor reports the
  party walking out as the regulars being "removed"; the wildcard then erased
  canon's record of where they live. Removal now drops residency only for the
  room the party is standing in.

The narrator was not the cause. Prose saying an authored resident is absent
("Mara Venn is nowhere in sight") appears in passing runs too — it does not
reach state, and the extractor's own `removed_npcs` on the departure turn was
handled correctly all along.

**A book can be compiled and installed.** `compile_sourcebook` /
`install_sourcebook` / `load_sourcebook` project an authored
`CampaignSourcebook` into canon, the graph and an opening scene. Visibility is
enforced on every channel — claims, quest text, relationships, hidden
inventory, NPC status — not just on claims.

**Secrets do not leak.** `lore_recall` grades a live run against the book.
Withheld canon stayed out of the prose in **9 of 9** runs, measured by canary
tokens that exist nowhere else in the book, script, or English.

**Asking for lore reaches it.** `get_scene_relevant_facts` anchored facts on
where the party STOOD while graph entity retrieval already seeded from what
the player SAID, so canon about a subject outside the room was unreachable
however directly it was asked for. Facts now carry a second anchor —
entities the action names — on its own prompt budget beside the scene's.

The graph's tier-1 match supplies the candidates but does not decide: it is
bare substring by design, because a speculative seed only costs an entity
card, whereas anchoring durable canon is a stronger claim. `entity_named_in_text`
(`game/identity.py`) is the gate both anchor paths share, and it wants two
things — a token-boundary hit, so "brambles" does not open Bram's file, and
an identity-bearing name, so a placeholder promoted to an alias ("the
innkeeper") cannot make one off-screen NPC permanent furniture in the prompt.

Measured at the turn that motivated it ("I ask Mara Venn what she knows
about the Ash Gate"), reading the assembled context out of the turn logs:

| | "Grey Hind" reaches the turn-1 prompt | `book_canon_reached_the_prompt` |
|---|---|---|
| before | **0 of 3** runs | 1 of 3 passed |
| after | **3 of 3** runs | **3 of 3** passed |

The "after" column is the merged code; turn-1 reach held **9 of 9** across
every draft of the change, including the looser pre-review one.
Walking to the gate later could still surface the fact on master, which is why
the run-level gate passed there once; the per-turn reading is the sharper
instrument and the one to trust. Prompt cost at the identical turn-1 state:
529 → 656 characters, one extra fact line. Per-run token totals sit inside
master's own spread — narrative divergence dominates.

**That table is all turn-1, and turn 1 is the easy case.** Both fact budgets
were recency slices (`[-max:]`), and authored canon is installed at turn 0, so
it held the oldest ledger positions and was always the first thing evicted —
irreversibly. Measured on the merged code, off-stage subject, action "I ask
about Old Bram": reached at 5 newer facts naming him, **gone at 6, and gone
for good**. A fresh ledger is exactly the regime where "most recently written"
and "true" agree, so the evidence above could not have caught this, and a long
campaign — where seeded lore is supposed to pay off — is where it bites.

The same slice inverted the scene budget: standing AT the Ash Gate, its
authored line is scene-matched, so 25 newer facts about the gate's *warden*
evicted it from the scene budget, and the action budget then excluded it for
being scene-*reachable*. Reachable by nothing. Asked from the tavern, the same
question reached it — **standing closer to a subject hid its canon.**

**Settled: provenance outranks recency, as a floor.** `WorldState.canon_facts`
records which established facts an install wrote; each budget is then spent
canon-first up to **half (rounded up), a floor and not a quota** — whatever
one side leaves unspent the other takes. Canon is taken in authored order
(stable turn to turn, so the prompt does not churn); play facts stay
newest-first, which is the right rule for events. Ledgers with no marking rank
exactly as they did.

The mark is written at exactly one seam — `_seed_opening_scene`, which is what
both `apply_sourcebook` and `install_sourcebook` install facts through — and it
round-trips in the world snapshot, so a resumed campaign does not forget which
facts are the book's. It is also *derivable*: `sourcebook_claim` joined to
`campaign_claim_state` is the system of record for what an install asserted, so
a ledger that predates the field can be re-marked from the bound key rather
than guessed at. Nothing needs that yet — no production path installs a book
(open item 2), so there are no unmarked seeded campaigns in existence.

Rejected: *canon always wins* (a book with 60 facts about a room would leave
the narrator describing it by the book while blind to the fight that just
ended in it — pinned by `test_canon_cannot_crowd_out_what_just_happened`), and
*loosen the scene-reachable exclusion* (it would let naming the NPC you are
already talking to silently widen the standing fact block from 20 to 26, which
`test_naming_someone_in_the_room_does_not_widen_the_scene_window` exists to
prevent). The canon floor fixes the inversion at its cause instead: the fact is
never evicted, so closer is now strictly better rather than worse. Play-written
chatter that ages out of the scene window still ages out — that is the window
working.

After: canon reached at **6, 12, 40 and 200** newer facts naming its subject,
and at every standpoint. `TestCanonOutranksRecency` pins it on deep ledgers,
and `test_seeded_canon_still_reaches_the_prompt_late_in_a_campaign` drives the
real `_build_context` 40 turns deep. Every "canon is reached" assertion is
paired with the same ledger unmarked; injecting the old ranking fails 6 of the
new pins, and injecting canon-always-wins fails 3 others.
**The graph is genuinely an index now.** Migration 007 gives authored content
canonical tables; `SourcebookRepository` imports a validated book atomically
and answers the overlay's questions as joins (undiscovered discoverable
claims, supersession resolved overlay-over-book, faction rosters, "hostile to
X" in the full 24-kind vocabulary, "authored in a region the party has not
touched"). `install_sourcebook` builds the indexes by reading the rows BACK
and refuses to install if they do not reproduce the book. A test deletes every
graph node and restores the world from a 64-character key.

## Open, ranked by impact

### 1. The sourcebook flow has never run against a real Discord client
It IS wired now: `/campaign create` takes an optional `sourcebook`,
`data/sourcebooks/` is the shelf, and `_handle_start` installs before the
opening narration. The install path, the library scan, the visibility
boundary and the roster guards are all pinned against a real database and a
real graph.

What is NOT proven is the Discord half — and the reason is narrower than
"no py-cord", which is what this doc said before someone checked:

| interpreter | py-cord | `discord.ApplicationContext` | imports the cog |
|---|---|---|---|
| system python (runs pytest) | 2.7.1 | no | **fails** |
| `venv` (runs mypy) | 2.8.0 | yes | ok |

So the cog IS importable and CAN take mocked-adapter tests — just not under
the interpreter the suite runs on. Aligning those two versions is the
prerequisite for testing this layer at all, and is worth doing before
writing the tests, not after.

Until then the option handling, autocomplete and interaction lifecycle rest
on assumption — and a review found `_handle_start` responding twice to an
already-deferred interaction, which would have crashed the whole flow
before the opening ever ran. That class of defect is invisible here. One
manual run through the wizard is still worth more than any test currently
in the suite.

Also unbuilt: a reset ABOVE `WorldStateStore`. `seed_opening_scene(force=)`
resets WorldState only; the graph, vector memory, scene registry and
campaign dead-NPC governance survive it. Campaign REPLACEMENT or replay
needs one that owns all of them.

### 2. Local models: unresolved, not disproven
`think` defaulting on made qwen3.5 return **empty** responses; fixing it took
a local lore run 728s → 116s with coherent prose. Still open after that fix:
Ollama `500 EOF` mid-run, and `hermes_tool_calls=0` (no tool calls emitted).
Both need clean runs on a quiet daemon. `TODO.md`'s first item — porting the
bookended prompt structure to the production narrator, which took
qwen3.5:27b from 50% → 88% on tool fidelity — is the obvious lever to try.

### 3. Harness loses ~1 run in 5 to a silent teardown death
All turns complete, then exit 1 with no traceback, empty stderr, no artifact.
Mitigated (artifacts are written *before* teardown, so a lost run is now a
recorded failure rather than a vanished one) but never root-caused. Suspect
native Chroma/SQLite shutdown. Matters because `soak_gate` scores only
artifacts that exist, so vanished runs used to *raise* the apparent pass rate.

### 4. Known-latent, currently harmless
- ~~**Residency erased on scene departure.**~~ **Closed 2026-07-26.** It was
  biting: the evidence the old note asked for arrived as a 2-of-3 gate
  failure. Both this and the paraphrase fork below were catalogued here as
  harmless *because the gate was passing* — which is the lesson worth
  keeping. A latent mechanism defect and a passing gate only coexist until
  something else changes the odds; here the third defect (batch re-ordering,
  invisible to this list) was already destroying the same edge by a
  different route.
- ~~**Location nodes fork on paraphrase.**~~ **Closed 2026-07-26** — see
  "Arriving somewhere restores its known inhabitants" above.
- **No location-fidelity gate.** A live run put Sable Quill in the tavern
  when the book places her at the Ash Gate. Gates assert authored residents
  are *present*, not that others are *absent*. Still open, and the residency
  fix widens it slightly: a scene-scoped removal deliberately keeps a
  residency edge that a campaign-wide one would have deleted, so an NPC who
  genuinely leaves on the same turn the party does can be hydrated back once.
  That is the cheap direction of the trade — visible and correctable, where
  a deleted edge is not recoverable — but it is the thing to watch.

## Method notes worth keeping

**Grade retrieval in the prompt, not the prose.** Two recall gates were built
wrong first: loose word tokens graded the narrator's vocabulary (it recalled
Old Bram by name every run but said "sealed" not "closed"), then unique
tokens graded whether it echoed an incidental detail. The graph's job ends
when context is assembled. Turn logs record it, so this is deterministic,
model-free, and free to repeat. Leakage stays a prose property — correctly,
since a secret in the output is a leak however phrased.

**Fakes hid a HIGH-severity regression.** The single-writer work shipped a
silent no-write because the unit fakes had no `equipped` semantics and no
`get_item_by_index`. Prefer real DB/repo/executor tests for anything that
resolves identity.

**Adversarial review earns its cost.** Three reviews this session found
defects in changes I was confident about, including two that were invisible
to the suite. On the sourcebook compiler, 9 findings survived and *none*
were refuted. The canonical-layer review ran five lenses and found a HIGH in
each of two of them — one caught independently by two reviewers, which is
what made it credible immediately.

**Green tests are not a measured suite.** Mutation testing scored the
canonical layer's first suite at **63%**: 40 plausible-wrong implementations
shipped without a single failure. The worst were the leak assertions, which
read `store.state.to_yaml()` — a view that emits only scene-relevant facts,
so a secret about somewhere else was filtered out *whether or not the
compiler leaked it*. An absence assertion needs a positive control proving
the check could have found something; without one it is decoration.

**Do not run mutation testing beside readers.** Five review agents shared one
worktree and one pytest tmp root. Two produced failures that looked like real
defects and were another agent's injected mutation or a tmp-dir collision;
one reviewer wisely validated against `git show HEAD:` instead of the working
tree. Give a mutation harness its own worktree.

**Do not call a capability verdict off one contaminated run.** "Local models
can't drive this harness" came from a single run whose turn 1 timed out —
and a turn-1 failure cascades, since no opening scene means every later turn
runs on broken context. The cause was a config default, not the model.
