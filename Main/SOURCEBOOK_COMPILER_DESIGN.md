# Sourcebook Compiler: Data Model and Authoring Protocol

## Decision

A sourcebook should be a versioned, provider-neutral package of canonical world data. It is not a large narrator prompt and it is not a dump of the live knowledge graph.

The sourcebook remains immutable after import. Play creates a runtime overlay of events, state changes, discoveries, and superseding claims. A compiler validates the package once, then projects it into the relational store, knowledge graph, and vector index. SQLite/sourcebook data is canonical; the graph and embeddings are rebuildable indexes.

The executable authoring contract is `dnd_bot/models/sourcebook.py`.

## What exists today

| Layer | Current strength | Current sourcebook limit |
|---|---|---|
| SQLite | Campaigns, sessions, characters, player inventory, NPC basics, player-to-NPC sentiment, memories, audit records | No canonical tables for locations, factions, world items, quests, timelines, secrets, or story structure |
| Knowledge graph | Persistent NPC/location/item/quest nodes and nine useful relation types | Node properties are untyped strings; only four entity types; no provenance, visibility, temporal validity, or rich relationship history |
| WorldState | Strong current-scene projection, stable NPC identity, quest snapshot, facts, flags, inventory transfers, governance | Deliberately compact and transient; unsuitable as a whole-world authoring model |
| Vector store | Rebuildable semantic retrieval for descriptions, memories, and narrative chunks | Cannot be authoritative, enforce referential integrity, or distinguish truth from rumor by itself |

Directly pouring a generated world into the existing graph would create avoidable failure modes: slug collisions, facts with no owner or time scope, DM-only secrets leaking into player context, duplicate item ownership, and no way to distinguish original canon from changes caused during play.

## Target truth layers

1. **Authored canon** — immutable `CampaignSourcebook` versions with stable IDs, provenance, private/public knowledge, histories, and cross-references.
2. **Campaign overlay** — append-only events and claims produced during play. Later facts supersede earlier facts; they do not silently rewrite history.
3. **Materialized state** — the current NPC status, ownership, location, quest state, clocks, and player knowledge derived from canon plus the overlay.
4. **Retrieval projections** — graph edges, embeddings, summaries, and current-scene prompt views. Every projection can be rebuilt from layers 1–3.

## Sourcebook v1 coverage

The v1 contract makes these concepts first-class:

- Location hierarchy from world/region down to room, plus explicit routes, travel cost, access rules, and hazards.
- NPC identity, status, public and private histories, behavior rules, goals, fears, voice, faction membership, significant inventory, and optional stat blocks.
- Creatures and reusable stat blocks.
- Significant items with history, mechanics, attunement, charges, uniqueness, and default placement.
- Factions with ideology, goals, methods, resources, leadership, ranks, headquarters, and territory.
- Cultures, religions, languages, laws, economies, magic systems, and other lore domains.
- Directed, typed relationships with valence, public/private descriptions, and event-linked history.
- Truth-bearing claims with canon status, visibility, who knows them, evidence, temporal validity, and contradiction groups.
- Ordered historical events with participants, locations, causes, and consequences.
- Quests with objective dependency graphs, reveals, rewards, expiry, and success/failure consequences.
- Story arcs with central questions, flexible beats, reveals, possible consequences, endings, and escalation clocks.
- Encounters with triggers, stakes, scaling, and noncombat solutions.
- A concrete starting state: opening situation, location, active quests/arcs, player-known claims, items, and clocks.

Pack validation currently rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, cyclic location containment, self-routes, custom relationships without names, and duplicate ownership of unique items.

## Provider-neutral worldbuilding protocol

Any capable chat model should use the same JSON Schema generated by `CampaignSourcebook.model_json_schema()`. Prompt prose may differ by provider; the artifact contract and gates do not.

The authoring session should proceed in bounded passes:

1. **Creative brief** — pitch, tone, themes, safety boundaries, play tier, and content budgets. No entities yet.
2. **World skeleton** — stable IDs for regions, settlements, factions, central tensions, and the main arc. Stubs only.
3. **Topology and history** — location containment/routes and a dated causal timeline. Validate reachability and chronology.
4. **Cast and institutions** — NPC/faction sheets, behavioral decision rules, histories, and relationship edges. Every named reference must be an ID.
5. **Material culture** — significant items, ownership/placement, lore domains, customs, laws, and recurring sensory motifs.
6. **Secrets and epistemology** — canonical claims, rumors, lies, evidence chains, who knows what, and reveal paths. This is what prevents omniscient NPCs and secret leakage.
7. **Playable structure** — quests, objectives, fronts/clocks, story beats, encounters, consequences, and multiple ending possibilities. Beats are opportunities, not a forced script.
8. **Adversarial review** — search for dangling references, duplicate people/items, impossible travel, dead characters assigned active roles, relationship asymmetry, circular prerequisites, unseeded reveals, and single points of narrative failure.
9. **Compile** — validate the final package, produce a deterministic manifest/hash, import in one transaction, build graph/vector projections, and run a smoke campaign from the starting state.

Each pass emits a small patch against stable IDs, never a wholesale rewrite of the book. The chat carries a compact manifest of IDs and unresolved review findings rather than the entire prose corpus. This lets the process survive context-window compaction and move between ChatGPT, Claude, local models, or another provider.

## Anti-derail rules for authoring models

- Create an entity stub and stable ID before writing facts that reference it.
- Structured canon and optional flavor prose are separate outputs.
- Never introduce a new proper noun inside a record without either referencing an existing ID or adding a stub in the same patch.
- Claims are atomic. Conflicting versions share a contradiction group and declare which version is canon, believed, disputed, or false.
- DM-only knowledge and player-known knowledge are separate fields, not prose conventions.
- A relationship is an edge with direction and history, not a sentence buried in two character biographies.
- A unique item's holder/placement has one canonical source of truth.
- Story beats state triggers and consequences; they do not assume a particular player choice.
- Generation budgets are explicit: minimum and maximum counts, relationship density, quest/secret coverage, and a limit on orphan entities.
- Deterministic validation decides whether a patch lands. An LLM reviewer can suggest repairs but cannot waive schema invariants.

## Compiler projection plan

The production compiler should be idempotent and transactional:

1. Parse and validate the full sourcebook offline.
2. Compute a canonical JSON hash and refuse an already-imported version unless explicitly replacing a draft.
3. Insert the immutable sourcebook version and normalized canonical records in a single SQLite transaction.
4. Create a campaign overlay whose base version points to that sourcebook hash.
5. Materialize initial NPC status, item ownership, locations, quests, claims, and clocks.
6. Project supported entities and edges into the knowledge graph using sourcebook IDs unchanged.
7. Embed only retrieval-safe renderings. DM-only claims receive visibility metadata and must never enter generic player-context retrieval.
8. Verify projection counts and sample round trips, then publish the campaign atomically.

If graph or vector projection fails, the import remains recoverable: rebuild projections from canonical SQLite records instead of asking a model to regenerate lore.

## Production schema work still required

The v1 authoring contract is ready; production import is intentionally not wired into the live database yet. The next implementation slice should add:

1. Sourcebook/version, canonical entity, relationship, claim, event, and overlay-event migrations.
2. Expanded graph entity/relation vocabularies and structured property values.
3. An importer with dry-run validation, atomic commit, projection receipts, and rollback.
4. Visibility- and time-aware graph/vector retrieval.
5. A materializer that derives current state from sourcebook plus overlay events.
6. Provider adapters that run the bounded authoring passes and emit schema-valid patches.
7. A compact quality report covering lore density, connectedness, quest/reveal reachability, relationship depth, contradictions, and orphan content.

That sequencing lets the creative protocol become ambitious without making generated prose a privileged database writer.

## Why canonical tables, and not a document blob

Recorded because the shortcut is tempting and wrong. Storing the validated
book as one versioned JSON document would give canonicality and a rebuild
path cheaply — and it fails the moment the runtime overlay needs to join
against it.

The purpose of pre-seeding is to give the realtime systems a coherent
foundation to build on. That makes the seeded corpus a *query target*, not
an archive. The queries play actually generates are not BFS-from-a-seed:

- Which DISCOVERABLE claims has this party not yet earned?
- Which claim currently supersedes which, once play overturns canon?
- Every member of a faction; every NPC hostile to X; everything authored in
  a region the party has not touched.
- What did the party learn, and when — overlay events joined to canon.

None of those are answerable against a blob without loading the whole book
and scanning it per turn, and all of them are cheap against indexed columns.

The graph cannot substitute either, by construction: node properties are
`dict[str, str]`, there are four entity types, and there is no provenance,
visibility, or temporal validity. `sourcebook_compiler` is deliberately a
LOSSY projection — 24 relationship kinds collapse onto 9 relation types, and
visibility is enforced by dropping content. Graph-only storage therefore
discards exactly the structure that makes seeded lore coherent.

**Column vs JSON rule.** Normalize what the overlay joins, filters or
traverses: locations, npcs, items, factions, quests, relationships, and
above all `claims` (subject_id, visibility, canon_status, superseded_by)
plus a per-campaign discovery table. Keep as JSON columns the sub-structures
that are read whole and never filtered — `BehaviorProfile`, sensory details,
authoring notes, story beats. This keeps the migration surface proportional
to what is genuinely queried, which is the real cost concern, rather than
mirroring all 25+ authoring classes.
